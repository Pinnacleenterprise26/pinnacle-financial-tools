What Gets Deducted From Your Paycheck

Gross pay is what you earn before anything is taken out. Net pay is what's left after federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions or health insurance are subtracted.

Federal Income Tax vs. FICA

Federal income tax uses progressive brackets, so different portions of taxable income are taxed at different rates. FICA is separate: Social Security is withheld at 6.2% up to the annual wage cap, while Medicare is withheld at 1.45% on all wages. Employers also withhold an additional 0.9% Medicare tax on wages above $200,000 during the calendar year.

How Pre-Tax Deductions Lower Your Tax Bill

Contributing to a traditional 401(k) reduces taxable income before federal tax is calculated, which is why raising your contribution often shrinks your paycheck by less than expected - part of it would've gone to taxes anyway.

Why Your Actual Paycheck May Differ

This gives a solid estimate, but real paychecks vary with state/local tax (not included here), Roth vs. traditional 401(k), HSA/FSA contributions, and bonus or overtime taxation. Treat this as a planning estimate.
